Chapter 2 • Hardware Basics

Topic 1: Input Devices & Peripherals

Manual vs. Automated Entry

What is a Peripheral Device?

A peripheral device is any extra piece of hardware plugged into or connected to the main computer system (CPU) so you can put information in, get information out, or save your work.

Two Ways to Enter Data

1

Manual Input Devices (You do the work)

Tools where a person has to type, click, tap, or speak to enter information.

Manual
Keyboard & Mouse

Used for typing documents and moving the cursor around the screen.

Manual
Touchscreen

Lets you tap directly on the screen using your finger or a stylus (detects either finger pressure or body heat).

Manual
Microphone

Captures sound and spoken words.

2

Direct / Automated Data Entry Devices (The machine reads it for you)

Fast scanners that read special codes or marks straight off a page or card without anyone typing.

Automated
Barcode & QR Code Reader

Uses a laser beam to scan black-and-white lines on store items to check prices and track stock.

Automated
Optical Mark Reader (OMR)

Reads shaded pencil bubbles on paper forms—used by CXC to mark Paper 1 multiple-choice exam sheets.

Automated
Optical Character Reader (OCR)

Scans printed paper text and turns it into editable words inside a word processor on your computer.

Automated
Magnetic Ink Character Reader (MICR)

Reads the special magnetic ink numbers printed along the bottom of bank cheques.

Automated
Magnetic Stripe & Smart Card Reader

Reads account details stored on the black strip or gold chip on bank and credit cards.

Automated
Biometric Scanner

Checks unique physical body features—like your fingerprint, palm print, or face—to unlock doors or devices.

Speech-to-Text vs. Voice-to-Text (Know the Difference!)

Speech-to-Text Speech Recognition

Focuses on WHAT words are being spoken

It turns spoken words into live written text on a screen. This helps people type hands-free and allows people who are deaf or hard of hearing to read what someone is saying.

Voice-to-Text Voice Recognition

Focuses on WHO is speaking

It listens to the unique sound of your specific voice (like a vocal fingerprint) to confirm your identity for security.

Chapter 4 • Data & Memory

Topic 2: Computer Storage Devices & Memory

Byte → KB → MB → GB → TB (1,024 Rule)

How We Measure Storage Space

Unit 1 • Smallest

Byte (B)

The smallest basic unit of storage. 1 Byte holds 1 single character (such as "A" or "$").

Unit 2 • Tiny

Kilobyte (KB)

Equal to 1,024 Bytes (about 1 thousand bytes). Holds a short plain-text email or a tiny document.

Unit 3 • Medium

Megabyte (MB)

Equal to 1,024 Kilobytes (about 1 million bytes). Holds a high-resolution photo or a short MP3 song.

Unit 4 • Large

Gigabyte (GB)

Equal to 1,024 Megabytes (about 1 billion bytes). Small flash drives and memory cards usually hold 1 to 64 GB.

Unit 5 • Massive

Terabyte (TB)

Equal to 1,024 Gigabytes (about 1 trillion bytes). Modern laptops and desktop computers use terabytes to store massive amounts of files.

Memory Rules (1,024 Step Ladder)
  • • 1 Kilobyte (KB) = 1,024 Bytes
  • • 1 Megabyte (MB) = 1,024 Kilobytes (KB)
  • • 1 Gigabyte (GB) = 1,024 Megabytes (MB)
  • • 1 Terabyte (TB) = 1,024 Gigabytes (GB)

Three Simple Ways to Group Storage

1

Internal vs. External

Internal storage is built right inside the computer case. External storage plugs in from the outside using a cable or wireless connection.

2

Primary vs. Secondary

Primary storage is built-in memory the computer must have to start up and run. Secondary storage is extra space used to keep your personal files, photos, and videos safe.

3

Volatile vs. Non-Volatile

  • • Volatile (Temporary): Forgetful memory. It needs electricity to hold data—if the power cuts off, everything inside is erased.
  • • Non-Volatile (Permanent): Safe memory. It keeps your files saved even when the computer is turned completely off.

Built-In (Internal) Memory Types

ROM (Read-Only Memory)

Primary Non-Volatile

Primary, non-volatile chip that holds the permanent startup instructions the computer needs just to wake up and turn on.

RAM (Random-Access Memory)

Primary Volatile

Primary, volatile working memory. It holds the apps and files you are actively working on right now.

Cache Memory

Primary Volatile

Super-fast, volatile helper memory that grabs frequently used information from RAM so the computer's brain (CPU) can reach it instantly.

IHDD (Internal Hard Disk Drive)

Primary Non-Volatile

Primary, non-volatile main storage drive inside the computer. It uses spinning magnetic platters to permanently save all your files and programs.

Extra (Secondary) & Online Storage Types

Secondary Storage

External Hard Disk Drive (EHDD)

A portable box with spinning magnetic disks inside that connects via USB cable. Holds huge amounts of data (1–10 TB), though dropping it can break the moving parts inside.

Secondary Storage

Solid State Drive (SSD)

Fast, lightweight storage that uses microchips instead of spinning disks (examples: USB flash drives, pen drives, and memory cards).

Secondary Storage

Optical Discs

Flat plastic discs read and written using laser light beams (CDs for music, DVDs for standard movies, Blu-ray Discs™ for high-definition video).

Network Storage

Network-Attached Storage (NAS)

Shared storage drives connected to a company's private network so workers in the same building can open the same files.

Cloud-Based Storage

Online Servers

Saving your files over the internet onto giant remote servers (such as Google Drive or Microsoft OneDrive).

Good Points

You can open your files from any phone or computer with internet, and your work stays safe if your laptop breaks.

Bad Points

You cannot reach your files without internet, and extra storage space often costs money.

Chapter 4 • Cyber-Care & Safety

Topic 3: Cyber-Security, Computer Care & Ergonomics

Data Protection & Desk Health

Protecting Computers from Inside Threats (Workplace & School)

Strong Passwords & PINs

Locking accounts and files so unauthorized people cannot open them.

Biometric Access Readers

Checking physical body traits before letting someone enter a room or system:

  • Unimodal: Checks one feature (like a fingerprint).
  • Multimodal: Checks two or more features together (like a palm print + voice print).

Blocking USB Ports

Turning off unused USB ports so people cannot plug in random flash drives to steal files or spread viruses.

Backup Power (UPS & Generators)

Using battery backup boxes so computers do not shut off abruptly during a power outage.

Protecting Computers from Outside Threats (The Internet)

Network Defense

Firewall

A digital security guard (hardware or software) that checks internet traffic coming in and going out, blocking hackers from entering.

Malware Defense

Antivirus Software

Programs that scan your computer to catch, block, and delete computer viruses.

System Maintenance

Software Updates (Patches)

Installing updates regularly to close security holes.

Healthy Computer Habits (Ergonomics)

Ergonomics means setting up your desk and equipment properly so your body stays comfortable and injury-free while working:

1

Screen Height

Place the top of your monitor at eye level so you do not strain your neck.

2

Chair & Posture

Sit in an adjustable chair that supports your lower back, with your feet flat on the floor or on a footrest.

3

Safe Cords

Keep electrical cords tucked neatly along walls so nobody trips over them.

4

The 20-20-20 Eye Rule

Every 20 minutes, look away at something 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds to rest your eyes.

Practical Preparation • CSEC SBA

Topic 4: Business Documents & SBA Portfolio Basics

Layouts & Portfolio Items

Key Document Styles & Layouts

Blocked-Style Business Letter

Every single line—including the date, inside address, paragraphs, and closing—starts flush against the left margin (no indents).

Open Punctuation

Leaving out commas at the end of the greeting (Dear Mr Clarke) and the sign-off (Yours faithfully).

Memorandum (Memo)

A short internal message sent between workers inside the same company using four standard headings: TO:, FROM:, DATE:, and SUBJECT:.

Official CSEC EDPM SBA Portfolio Documents to Know

SBA Item 1

Circular Letter with Tear-Off Slip

One standard letter sent out to many customers, with a small return section at the bottom they can fill out and send back.

SBA Item 2

Notice of Meeting & Agenda

An announcement telling members when and where a meeting will happen, paired with a numbered list of topics to be discussed.

SBA Item 3

Minutes of a Meeting

The official written record of what was discussed and decided during a meeting.

SBA Item 4

Tabular Work

Neat rows and columns used for price lists, invoices, or financial statements (using spreadsheet formulas like =SUM() to add totals).

SBA Item 5

Display Work

Creative, eye-catching documents such as invitations, restaurant menus, or event programmes.

SBA Item 6

Legal Documents

Formal binding agreements such as a Contract of Employment or a Lease Agreement.
